# FloodTracer — NASA Space Apps 2026 · team "NASA Que Ver" (Montevideo, Uruguay)

**An interactive flood explorer powered by NISAR.** Challenge: *Dancing with the SARs* (`docs/challenge.md`).

With the **NISAR** radar (L-band, NASA/ISRO), which sees through clouds, we followed the storm of **15–19 August 2026** in eastern Uruguay and measured where water appeared, how far it reached and **how long it stayed**. The case study is **northern Rocha**; the explorer adds Treinta y Tres, the Laguna Merín and Río Branco.

## 1. View the app

`app/` is a static three-page site with a shared menu (**Story · Explore · How we did it** + EN/ES) on top of `app/data/` (versioned). It needs neither Python nor the NISAR data, but it **does need an http server** and internet access (MapLibre, base maps and fonts).

```bash
python3 -m http.server -d app 8000     # from the repository root
# Story:           http://localhost:8000/              (?lang=es for Spanish)
# Explore:         http://localhost:8000/explorer.html (?zona=rocha_norte|treinta_y_tres|merin_arrozales|rio_branco_jaguarao)
# How we did it:   http://localhost:8000/method.html
```

- **Story** (`index.html`, `story.js`, `story.css`): eight slides about northern Rocha, no scrolling.
- **Explore** (`explorer.html`, `explorer.js`, `explorer.css`): the four areas, with water by date, where the water stayed longest, date comparison or map versus radar, and roads.
- **How we did it** (`method.html`, `method.js`, `method.css`): the eleven steps of the method with real images of northern Rocha.

**No figure is written by hand**: everything comes from `app/data/manifest.json` (`scripts/exportar_app.py` plus the `metodo` entry from `scripts/exportar_metodo.py`). Check: `python scripts/check_story_literals.py`. GitHub Pages publishes `app/` on every push to `main` (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`).

## 3. Setup (once)

Tested on macOS with Python 3.11 and GDAL 3.13.

1. Free account at https://urs.earthdata.nasa.gov/ and credentials in `~/.netrc` (outside the repository):
   ```bash
   printf 'machine urs.earthdata.nasa.gov\n    login YOUR_USER\n    password YOUR_PASSWORD\n' > ~/.netrc && chmod 600 ~/.netrc
   ```
   If the download returns `401`/`403`, authorize *Alaska Satellite Facility Data Access* in Earthdata → *Applications*.
2. Command-line GDAL: `brew install gdal` (with GDAL < 3.13 the `NETCDF:` driver is used automatically).
3. Python: `python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate && pip install -r requirements.txt`.

## 4. Pipeline: from download to app

From the repository root, with the venv active. **Without `--zona`**: with `--zona` the scripts do not write the global CSV files that `exportar_app.py` reads.

| # | Command | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `python scripts/descargar_zonas.py descargar --zona todas --workers 4` | GCOV crops (HH/HV) per area and track in `data_nisar/zonas/` (only the crop of each scene, via `/vsicurl/`) |
| 2 | `python scripts/capas_contexto.py` | OpenStreetMap (roads, towns, rivers), HydroBASINS and ESA WorldCover per track |
| 3 | `python scripts/clima.py` | ERA5 wind and rain at the time of each pass (Open-Meteo, no key), point over inland water |
| 4 | `python scripts/metricas_evento.py` | per track: permanent water, new water per date, pre-rain water, persistence |
| 5 | `python scripts/combinar_tracks.py` | combines the tracks of each area: confirmed extent (≥ 2 observations), pre-rain water, retreat |
| 6 | `python scripts/metricas_evento.py` (again) | removes the pre-rain water seen by the combination |
| 7 | `python scripts/combinar_tracks.py` (again) | keeps the CSV files consistent |
| 8 | `python scripts/armonizar_tracks.py` | agreement between tracks at most one day apart (Jaccard) |
| 9 | `python scripts/sensibilidad.py` | extent range with other detector settings |
| 10 | `python scripts/monte_ribereno.py` | DEM, HAND and water under riverside forest |
| 11 | `python scripts/agregar_impacto.py` | roads, land cover and impact per area |
| 12 | `python scripts/validar_s1.py` | comparison with Sentinel-1 (validation only; the first run downloads crops, which are cached) |
| 13 | `python scripts/agua_conocida.py` | check against JRC Global Surface Water (validation only) |
| 14 | `python scripts/caso_rocha_norte.py` | case-study checks (prints only) |
| 15 | `python scripts/exportar_optico.py --zona rocha_norte` | Sentinel-2 context photo for the Story (cached) |
| 16 | `python scripts/exportar_app.py` | regenerates `app/data/` (except `app/data/metodo/`) |
| 17 | `python scripts/exportar_metodo.py` | How we did it panels; **always after** `exportar_app.py` |

With the data already downloaded, steps 2–17 take about 15 minutes (plus the first run of `validar_s1.py`). To work only with northern Rocha you need `data_nisar/zonas/rocha_norte/` and `data_nisar/zonas/_clima/rocha_norte.json` (~1 GB): ask the team for them or download with `descargar_zonas.py descargar --zona rocha_norte`.

## 5. Method (summary; the details are in How we did it)

- **Product:** NISAR L2 GCOV (gamma0 RTC, 20 m), HH and HV. A 5×5 moving average against speckle. One series per track (same geometry); tracks are combined only as water masks.
- **Each pixel's normal:** median of the passes before the storm (before the global data gap, 27 Jul–10 Aug).
- **Permanent water:** darker than the track's calibrated threshold on most of the **calm** baseline passes (ERA5 wind < 3 m/s). The **ocean** is masked out as no data.
- **New water:** a drop of more than 3 dB against the normal (after removing the scene-wide shift) and HH < −14 dB, outside permanent water, in patches of at least 10 pixels.
- **Pre-rain water:** what was already wet on passes taken before the rain started (by time of pass) is shown apart (cyan) and not counted.
- **Confirmed extent:** new water seen on at least 2 passes (any track) between 15 Aug and 10 Sep. **Persistence:** interval between passes, its midpoint is used; retreat = two dry passes in a row. From October, cropland is no data (field preparation).
- **Excluded tracks:** `zonas.excluir_tracks` (today T132A in northern Rocha: permanent water from a single calm pass, 57% of the JRC water). They are processed for diagnosis but do not enter the combination or the app.
- **Validation (never part of the calculation):** Sentinel-1, agreement between tracks, JRC known water, the detector's noise floor (an area is not conclusive if the flooded area is less than twice the noise).

## 7. Team rules

1. NISAR is the main source. Other sources may improve the detection if the improvement is measured (ERA5 wind to pick calm passes). Validation sources (Sentinel-1, JRC) never enter the calculation and are never used for training.
2. One track per time series; tracks are combined only at the mask level.
3. Do not mix PROVISIONAL and VALIDATED products without normalizing (a jump of about −1.7 dB in HH).
4. Press figures are preliminary and shown with their source.

## 8. Current results

| Area | Flooded (≥ 2 obs.) | Already wet before | Half dry | Typical persistence | Roads under water |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Northern Rocha | 9,373 ha | 4,936 ha | day 25 (49.6% on 9 Sep, at the edge of the cut) | 18 days (12–24) | 4.8 km |
| Laguna Merín | 47,449 ha | 27,591 ha | day 25 | 12 days (6–18) | 41.9 km |
| Treinta y Tres | 6,303 ha | 3,093 ha | day 20 | 10 days (1–19) | 13.3 km |
| Río Branco | 2,070 ha | 440 ha | day 25 | 18 days (12–24) | 3.0 km |

Northern Rocha against Sentinel-1: NISAR finds 79% of the water Sentinel-1 sees (F1 0.63); agreement between tracks 0.72 (5 pairs, both passes after the rain started); it recognizes 96% of the JRC permanent water. The current figures always live in `app/data/manifest.json`.

## 9. Limitations

- 12-day revisit per track: durations are ranges, not hours.
- Wind: 2 of the 3 storm passes of the main track were windy (> 3 m/s), which brightens water; those passes are flagged, not corrected.
- Towns (double bounce) and raised rural roads confuse the radar; a road in a flooded area is not always a cut road.
- Data arrives days later: useful for response and planning, not for early warning.
- **Next steps:**
  - Normalize the signal between tracks to build a more complete timeline with all orbits.
  - Improve the water detection algorithm with machine learning (for example, a model trained on hand-labelled examples that also uses HV and each pixel's time series), keeping Sentinel-1 and JRC for validation only.
