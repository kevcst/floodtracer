NISAR L-band radar · Uruguay · flood of 15–19 August 2026

How long does the water stay?

Clouds blinded optical satellites during the August storm. NISAR's L-band radar did not. Every 12 days it mapped where new water appeared, how far it spread and when the land dried again, from the rice fields of the Laguna Merín basin to the forests along the Río Negro.

The event

One storm, two watersheds

The Cuchilla Grande splits Uruguay's east. Rain falling on the Atlantic side drains through the Olimar, Cebollatí and Yaguarón into the Laguna Merín. Rain on the other side runs down the Yí into the Río Negro. The same storm produced crests days apart. The six study areas follow that water.

Timeline (press and SINAE reports, used only as context)

  1. ~23 JulRío Yí reaches 8.95 m in Durazno; 57 people leave their homes.
  2. 27 Jul – 10 AugGlobal NISAR data gap. July passes serve as the "normal" baseline.
  3. 15–19 AugINUMET warning: 70–100 mm/day, over 250 mm in five eastern departments. 317 mm at Laguna Merín.
  4. 19–20 AugOlimar crests at ~8.6 m in Treinta y Tres; ~1,600 people displaced in the city.
  5. 22 AugYí crests at 9.75 m in Durazno, three days after the Olimar. National peak: 2,229 displaced.
  6. 23 AugTreinta y Tres drops to 418 displaced while Durazno rises to 416: the flood moves west.
  7. ~21–25 AugSoriano prepares for the Río Negro at Mercedes. El Niño expected to last until at least May 2027.

What L-band adds

Water under the trees on the Río Negro

When a forest floor floods, radar bounces between water and trunks and returns stronger. Optical satellites and C-band radar mostly miss this. At Mercedes, riverside forest brightens by 1–2 dB after the storm on all three tracks. Forest on higher ground brightens less, so the extra signal points to water rather than just wet soil.

For the team

Is this the idea to pursue?

Everything on this page comes from NISAR GCOV data we already downloaded (226 clips, June to October 2026, PROVISIONAL). Press and SINAE figures are context only.

Works today
  • The August flood shows clearly in the Laguna Merín basin and Treinta y Tres, with rise, peak and retreat, on three independent orbit tracks.
  • Retreat is measurable: in the rice fields 82% of the flooded area was wet on 28 Aug, 20% on 21 Sep and 8% on 15 Oct.
  • L-band sees flooding under riverside forest at Mercedes, which open-water maps miss.
  • The story is local and verifiable: SINAE displacement counts, river levels and INUMET warnings line up with the radar dates.
Still weak
  • Time under water has 12-day resolution. Combining tracks should give ~6 days, but tracks disagree: passes one day apart overlap only 17–40% at the peak. Thresholds need harmonizing first.
  • Durazno and Melo show almost no open-water signal. The forest signal there does not separate from general wetness.
  • Cities confuse radar (buildings also bounce the signal), so urban flooding in Treinta y Tres is under-counted.
  • Data arrives days after each pass. This is post-event assessment and planning, not early warning.
Numbers behind this page

New open water = HH backscatter below a per-track threshold (−17.5 to −18.7 dB, calibrated on permanent water) that was not water in most June–July passes. 5×5 speckle filter (~100 m). Maximum extent = any new water between 10 Aug and 10 Sep.
